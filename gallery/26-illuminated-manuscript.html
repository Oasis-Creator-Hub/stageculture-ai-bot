Here beginneth the seventh hour

Of the Owl that kept the Abbey’s Clock

nce, in the vale of Saint Orrin-under-Fell, there dwelt an owl of sober habit, who, seeing that the brothers’ great clock ran slow in frost and quick in thaw, took upon herself the keeping of the hours. Nota: the clock was a gift, & ill-made; the owl was neither. At Matins she cried once, softly, lest the novices be frighted; at Lauds twice; and so through the night, never erring, though no man had taught her number.

Now the Sacristan, a man named Brother Aldwyn, who loved his sleep above his psalter, complained to the Abbot, saying: It is not meet that a fowl should rule the house. And the Abbot answered him: Then rule it better, and she shall hold her peace. Whereat Brother Aldwyn was silent for the space of one whole winter. Some copies say two winters. They flatter him.

It is further written that the snail, being slow, is never late, for he sets out the day before; and that the hare, being swift, hath never yet heard the end of a sermon. Consider these things, reader, and wind thy clock.

Touch the creatures in the margin; they have opinions.
