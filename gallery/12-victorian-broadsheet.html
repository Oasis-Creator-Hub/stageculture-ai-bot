Mechanical Owl Elected to Parish Council

Astonishing scenes at the Corn Exchange. — Clockwork candidate carries the vote by forty-one to nine. — Vicar demands recount; owl declines to blink. — Shares in brass rise sharply.

Alarming Scarcity of Fog

Citizens urged to remain indistinct

The Harbour Board confirmed yesterday that reserves of municipal fog have fallen to their lowest ebb since records were first mislaid. For eleven consecutive mornings the far side of Tallow Street has been plainly visible, to the distress of residents on both.

Mr. Ambrose Thwick, Inspector of Vapours, attributes the shortage to “excessive clarity blowing in off the Continent” and has petitioned for a second kettle. Meanwhile the Society for Mysterious Assignations reports attendance down by two-thirds, members complaining that they can see precisely whom they are meeting.

A Balloon Goes Missing, With Vicar

The pleasure balloon Perseverance slipped her moorings at the Michaelmas Fête and was carried east at a dignified pace, the Reverend Octavius Pollifax still aboard and mid-sermon. Fishermen off Gull Sands report hearing the Second Lesson pass overhead at about four o'clock.

A reward of two guineas is offered for the balloon. Enquiries concerning the vicar may be addressed to his sister.

Not since the Great Marmalade Riot has Port Wexcombe known such an evening. At a quarter past nine the Returning Officer, visibly shaken, declared that the vacant seat for Belfry Ward had been won by a brass owl of some eighteen inches, constructed by Mrs. Hester Vane, horologist, of Cog Lane, and entered upon the ballot under the name Mr. T. Wit-Towoo.

The owl's campaign was, by every account, a model of restraint. It made no speeches. It promised nothing. On being asked its position upon the drainage question it rotated its head fully about and regarded the questioner for a period of two minutes, which the hall received with prolonged applause.

Its opponent, Alderman Josiah Brisket, who has held the seat for nineteen years and attended on four occasions, described the result as “an outrage against Nature and the rate-payer alike.” He was observed afterward attempting to bribe the victor with a mouse.

Mrs. Vane, addressing the crowd from an upturned crate, assured electors that the Councillor requires winding but once a week, takes no expenses, and “has never yet fallen asleep in committee, which is more than can be said for the mahogany.”

The Rev. Mr. Pollifax's formal objection — that the owl cannot take the oath — was set aside when the bird, being tapped smartly upon the crown, struck twelve. The Clerk ruled this sufficient.

The new member will take its perch on Tuesday. A small ladder has been ordered.

Letters to the Editor

Sir, — I wish to protest in the strongest terms against the new gas lamp opposite my bedroom. I have not been properly in the dark since February and am beginning to form views. — Insomniac, Tallow Street.

Sir, — Your correspondent of last week is mistaken. The noise heard nightly beneath the pier is not a sea-serpent but my husband, practising the euphonium where I have told him to. — Mrs. E. Garrowby.

Court & Curious

The Dowager Lady Trembleton has returned from taking the waters at Bad Humour, and is no better.

A pig belonging to Mr. Dunnock of Low Farthing was on Monday seen reading the notices outside the Assize Court. It has since left the district.

The Town Clock, slow these forty years, is now so far behind as to be correct again. The Corporation takes full credit.
