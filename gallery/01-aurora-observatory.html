Tonight's watchQuiet until eleven, then the curtain.

A fast stream left the sun three days ago and reaches the magnetosphere after dusk. Expect a pale arc low in the north, rising to moving bands and — if the field tips south — a full corona overhead. Drag the Kp dial above to see what each level looks like from our ridge.

InstrumentsThree ways of paying attention.

  1. The Hallan all-sky camera

    A fisheye eye under a heated glass blister. One exposure every eleven seconds since 1994 — over twenty million frames of the same patch of dark.

  2. Fluxgate magnetometer “Old Needle”

    Buried two metres under the moraine, away from every nail and stove. It feels a substorm half a minute before our eyes do.

  3. The 40 cm Brekke reflector

    Not for aurora at all. On still nights we open the slit, turn south, and let visitors find Saturn for themselves.
