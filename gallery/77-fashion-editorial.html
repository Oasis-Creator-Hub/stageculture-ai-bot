Chapter one

Squall

The first rule of the headland is that nothing stays where you pin it. Achterberg, who has dressed duchesses and one very patient greyhound, arrived with a trunk of wool and a roll of gaffer tape, and by Tuesday had surrendered the tape.

What remained was cut: shoulders built like sea walls, hems weighted with lead shot, a cobalt gabardine that turns the colour of a bruise when it rains — and it rained. “You don’t style weather,” he says. “You negotiate.”
