A story in five folds

The Valley Cut from One Sheet

Told with scissors by Grandmother Ilka, on a kitchen table, the winter the snow came up to the letterbox.

Scroll to unfold · move to peek between the layers
Fold one

First, the far hills

She folded the evening in half and cut along the crease. When she opened it there were mountains — pale ones, the kind that are mostly distance.

“Never cut the far things sharply,” she said. “They haven't decided what they are yet.”

Fold two

Then the pines

Small triangles, snipped quick, stood up in rows along the ridge. The scraps she blew off the table became the birds.

“A tree is only a mountain that chose to stay close.”

Fold three

Then the houses

Nine houses, and one of them crooked. She never fixed the crooked one.

“Somebody has to live differently,” she said, “or a village is just a pattern.”

Fold four & five

Last of all, the light

When the sun slid down behind the paper she took a pin and pricked a hole through every window.

Hold this page up to a lamp tonight. Someone in the crooked house is still awake — reading, I think, about you.
