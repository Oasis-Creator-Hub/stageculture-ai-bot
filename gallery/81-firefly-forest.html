Field notes · Hollin Wood · late June

The hour of small lanterns

Stand still long enough and the dark stops being empty. First one, low in the bracken. Then the whole understorey begins to breathe in green.

They are not drawn to noise. Move your hand slowly, as you would near a sleeping animal, and they will come to see what kind of warmth you are.

The old foresters called them wick-flies, and counted them the way other men count sheep — never reaching the end, never minding.

Somewhere above the canopy the moon is doing its large, cold work. Down here the light is small enough to hold a conversation with.

By midnight the dew will ground them. Until then, every signal is a question, and the wood is full of patient answers.
