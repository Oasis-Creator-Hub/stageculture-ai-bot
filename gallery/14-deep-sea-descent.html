Descent

The Verrin Trench is 9,840 metres of water stacked on water. Let go of the ladder. We will fall through five worlds, and the last light you see will not be the sun's.

Scroll to sink
Zone I · 0 – 200 m

The Sunlight Shallows

Everything you think of as “the sea” lives in this thin bright skin. Light pours down in columns; the water is warm enough to forget about.

Shoals of sunstitch minnows turn together like a single thought, flashing silver, then vanishing edge-on.

Zone II · 200 – 1,000 m

The Twilight Shelf

The blue thins to a rumour. Down here, colour is something animals make for themselves — small lanterns carried against an enormous dusk.

A lantern veil jelly pulses past, unhurried, trailing eight metres of stinging thread like the hem of a gown.

Zone III · 1,000 – 4,000 m

The Midnight Water

No sunlight has ever reached this water. Not once. The dark is not an absence here; it is the medium, like air.

Then a single green star swings toward you on a stalk. Behind it, slowly, a mouth assembles itself out of the black.

Zone IV · 4,000 – 6,000 m

The Abyssal Plain

Four hundred atmospheres. Two degrees above freezing. A flat grey desert of ooze, wider than continents, where a single footprint would last a thousand years.

Something soft opens above you like an umbrella in a doorway, considers the matter, and closes again.

Zone V · 6,000 – 9,840 m

The Hadal Floor

The trench narrows to a seam. Here the planet leaks: chimneys of black mineral breathe water hot enough to melt lead, and around them — against all sense — a garden.

You have run out of down. Above you is the height of a mountain range, made entirely of sea. Look up anyway.
